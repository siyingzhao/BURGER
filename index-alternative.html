Motivation

One instruction,
many valid routes.

A policy must learn the alternatives a task allows. Averaging different stroke sequences can produce a path unlike any demonstration. We compare BURGER and LIBERO using the same clustering procedure.

BURGER averages 7.13 effective modes per instruction, versus 1.09 for LIBERO—a 6.6× difference. For 31 of LIBERO’s 40 instructions, all paths fall in one cluster.

How routes are counted

40 instructions × approximately 42 demonstrations per dataset, including all four LIBERO suites. Each planar path is resampled to 32 points. A shared k-means codebook groups execution routes.

Effective mode count is the exponential of cluster entropy, Neff = exp(−Σj pj log pj). It measures trajectory diversity, not task success.

What the benchmark reveals

Even a circle separates the models.

The training data varies starting points and directions around one circle. Models differ in both execution and how much of that variation they retain.

Learned policies in simulation. One example per setting; the findings below use 20 rollouts each.

Longer chunks help execution. OFT makes contact in 6/20 rollouts at chunk length 1, and 20/20 at length 8.

A coherent circle can still hide a bias. π0-FAST draws clockwise in 95% of rollouts, compared with 56% of demonstrations.

How drawing quality changes with more data

We vary training classes from 10 to 132 and demonstrations per class from 10 to 100. More data helps π0 and π0.5 more consistently; π0-FAST shows more variable trends.

The score measures similarity to the target object class in a learned sketch embedding.

How we evaluate drawings

We train a projection head on frozen DINOv3 features to score drawings against their target class. In a study of 10 single-object classes, 66 participants ranked five drawings per class. The metric agrees more closely with their judgments than CLIPScore.

Learning a distribution of behaviors

One instruction,
more than one way to draw.

Helmets can differ in shape and stroke order. After training, π0.5 also produces different drawings for the same instruction.

Across four representative tasks, π0.5 retains 3.6–5.1 effective trajectory modes per instruction, compared with about 7.5 in the training data.

Scope and limitations

BURGER studies line drawing with a rigid tool. It simplifies the contact physics of painting and manipulation. Generated sketches and stroke-order heuristics shape the data distribution.

The evaluated policies can compose known objects, but struggle to draw unseen object categories. Drawing performance does not establish general manipulation ability.
